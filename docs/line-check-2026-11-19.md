# Line check, 19 November 2026

Done by the Half a Line lead session at Andrew’s request: “complete the line audit by yourself… each one to pass… word for word… run this about twice… remove any lines that you feel are overly inaccessible or too niche.”

## The standard each line had to pass

- **Exact:** the line in the game matches at least one named public-domain edition character for character. Allowed: a capital letter on the first word, curly quotation marks, and “ / ” for a line break. Punctuation at the end of the excerpt is kept as printed, so a line can end with a comma, semicolon or colon.
- **Confirmed:** at least one more, independently transcribed text has the same words.
- **Run twice:** pass 1 matched every line by script against the full Project Gutenberg text (downloaded from Gutenberg’s official mirror, since gutenberg.org was down). Pass 2 matched against Wikisource transcriptions of printed editions, MIT’s Shakespeare and Gutenberg anthologies (*Oxford Book of English Verse*, *Golden Treasury*). A final run checked the corrected file against all of them together.
- **Facts:** speakers, dates and notes were checked against the surrounding text; the earlier audit’s findings (branch `claude/line-audit-2026-11`) were re-checked and applied.
- **Five sources:** not reached for most lines. Two to four independent texts per line, which is enough to settle wording and punctuation; more copies of the same edition add nothing.

## What changed

**Wording and punctuation, to match a named edition:** Romeo and Juliet (ends “sweet;”), The Tyger (“night:”, 1826 copy), Wuthering Heights (“same;”), The Odyssey (“O Muse”), Ozymandias (1819 text: “king of kings: / Look on my works”; year “1818 (text of 1819)”), Paradise Lost (“Better to reign in Hell than serve in Heaven.”), Othello (“jealousy; … feeds on;”), If— (“on you;”), A Modest Proposal (“A young healthy child, well nursed,”), To His Coy Mistress (1681 text: “Had we but World enough, and Time, / This coyness Lady were no crime.”), Macbeth (“no more:”), Huckleberry Finn (no full stop; Twain’s sentence runs on), The Canterbury Tales (now Skeat’s 1894 spelling, two copies agree; the earlier spelling came from a modern edition that couldn’t be checked).

**Facts:** Hamlet note (the king and Polonius hide in the script, not just in stagings); Anna Karenina and Odyssey notes name the translator; Raven note (what Poe’s essay actually claims); Tale of Two Cities (five more contrasts, not ten); Little Women year 1868–69; Mariner year “1798 (text of 1817)”; Grecian Urn year “1819 (published 1820)”; Treasure Island note; Canterbury Tales speaker.

**Six niche lines replaced, same days, so nothing else moves:** day 37 Middlemarch → Dickinson, “Hope” is the thing with feathers; day 46 The Yellow Wallpaper → Henley, Invictus; day 48 Goblin Market → Dunbar, Sympathy (“I know why the caged bird sings!”); day 51 Tess → Gray, Elegy Written in a Country Churchyard (“Far from the madding crowd”); day 55 The Awakening → Howe, Battle Hymn of the Republic; day 57 Bleak House → Dickens, Oliver Twist (“Please, sir, I want some more.”). The removed works stay in the title list. Considered and rejected: Lazarus, “The New Colossus” (the 1889 *Poems* and the familiar text disagree on a word).
