## How the checks were done, and why most rows have fewer than 5 sources

The brief asked for at least 5 independent sources per line. **That bar was not met.** Each line was matched against 1 to 4 full texts fetched during this audit: 2 lines against 4 sources, 27 against 3, 19 against 2 and 12 against 1. The reasons:

- **gutenberg.org was down for maintenance.** The full texts were read from its official mirror (aleph.pglaf.org) and matched word for word by script. Links below point to the normal gutenberg.org pages.
- **Poetry Foundation and Folger can't be read automatically.** Poetry Foundation shows a bot check, which was not bypassed. Folger loads its text with scripts. They are listed as references for Andrew to click, not as checks.
- **The second source is Wikisource.** Its search was run on a distinctive phrase from each line. A row counts a Wikisource page only if the phrase appears in that page's text and the page is the right work. Wikisource often transcribes a different printing from Gutenberg (for example the 1813 Pride and Prejudice, the 1847 Jane Eyre, the 1798 Lyrical Ballads and the 1862 Goblin Market), which is how several edition differences were found.
- **Facts in notes and hints** (dates, who says what to whom, what happens next) were checked against the fetched texts where the text settles them: the next lines of the scene, stage directions, chapter placement. Outside facts (publication history, the Poe essay, Hallam's age, Dickens's "favourite child" preface, Young E. Allison's later ballad) come from standard reference knowledge and were not re-fetched. Their confidence labels say so.
- 12 lines rest on a single fetched text. All 12 are among the most-quoted lines in English, and each matched that text word for word. More sources would add little, but this is less than the brief asked for.

Counts are in the "Matched" figure of each row. Wordings that differ only in modern spelling (Gutenberg's "Tiger, tiger" for Blake, Skeat's "Aprille… sote" for Chaucer) are noted next to the source. The game follows Blake's own 1794 plates and the Riverside Chaucer, the standard teaching text.
